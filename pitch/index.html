THE PITCH

A life sim about the other side of the card.

Games about trading cards are about winning with them. Minthaven is about everything around that: tearing the pack open by hand, chasing a first print, arguing about centering, grading the card you're scared to sleeve — and selling the one you actually liked, because rent exists.

WHAT YOU DO

Wake up, read the market on your phone, walk into town, buy a pack, open it with your own hands. Trade with people who remember you. Sit down and play a real card game. Sleep, read the day's ledger, do it again.

WHY IT'S DIFFERENT

The card game inside the game is a genuine card game — 20-card decks, 2–5 minute matches, a tournament ladder. And the card market moves whether you're watching or not.

WHO IT'S FOR

The Stardew audience that wants a warm daily loop with a goal, and the collector audience raised on pack-opening videos who can't afford the real hobby.

MARKET

Three games proved the demand. None of them made this one.

TCG CARD SHOP SIMULATOR · 2024

Proved that “cards + simulation” is a very large, very underserved appetite.

WHAT IT LEFT ON THE TABLE

You play the shopkeeper. Its most common complaint is that you can't play the cards. We put you on the collector's side of the counter — and the card game is real.

POKÉMON TCG POCKET

Proved that the pack-opening ritual alone can carry an entire product.

WHAT IT LEFT ON THE TABLE

It's free-to-play gacha reaching for a real credit card. We keep the ritual and attach it to a single-player economy you can never pay into.

STARDEW VALLEY

Proved the cozy daily loop with long-horizon goals, and built the audience that still wants more of it.

WHAT IT LEFT ON THE TABLE

Crops. We swapped the farm for a binder: the same “one more day” rhythm, aimed at a collection instead of a harvest.

THE COLLECTOR

Grew up on Pokémon cards, watches pack-opening videos, can't afford the real hobby. Wants completion without guilt. Held by: the binder, set rewards, grading, the display case.

THE COMPETITOR

Hearthstone and Marvel Snap in their history. Wants short, deep matches and deckbuilding. Held by: the duel meta, rank, the World Championship.

THE COZY PLAYER

400 hours in Stardew, curious about cards but not committed. Held by: town life, NPCs, decorating, fishing. Can finish the game without ever duelling — collecting and trading are a complete path.

FEATURES — THE RITUAL

A pack is a ritual, not a button.

YOU OPEN IT, THE GAME DOESN'T

You drag the foil apart yourself, then flip the cards one by one. The glow tells you before the art does, and the sound tells you before the glow. Four pack types, a guaranteed hit slot, and pity counters the game never mentions.

RELOADING WON'T SAVE YOU

Every pull is seeded to your save. What you opened is what you opened — which is exactly what makes a good pull worth telling someone about.

07 / 15

FEATURES — THE OBJECT

One finite set. Every creature is exactly one card.

A SET YOU CAN ACTUALLY FINISH

Elementa is a single set — 75 cards, five elements, six rarity tiers, no seasonal drip. A binder page is a place you remember, not a row in a spreadsheet.

CONDITION, CENTERING, GRADING

Cards run Mint to Damaged, with a centering you can't see until you pay to find out. $25 and three days at the lab buys a number that can triple the price — and a slabbed card cannot be played. Cracking it back open is a decision, not a menu item.

08 / 15

FEATURES — THE PEOPLE

A price is something you can be wrong about.

THE MARKET MOVES WITHOUT YOU

Weekly waves, meta reports that lift a whole archetype overnight, news that guts the card you were saving for Friday. And nothing sells from the binder — every channel is a place you walk to, each paying differently, each open on different days.

PEOPLE REMEMBER

Twelve traders, each with an element they overpay for and a habit you can learn. Rip somebody off and it works. Once. The grudge turns up a few days later, when you need them.

09 / 15

FEATURES — THE GAME AND THE TOWN

And then you actually play the cards.

A REAL CARD GAME, NOT A MINIGAME

20-card decks, three creature slots, an energy curve, seven keywords and a five-element advantage circle. Matches run two to five minutes. No turn limit — run out of deck and you lose. Four AI tiers building from six real archetypes, and a ladder from the local Cup to the World Championship.

A TOWN THAT KEEPS GOING

Nine locations, twenty-five townsfolk on real routines, weather, and a light that slides from morning to evening while you stand there deciding. Fish the lake, decorate the house, chase gossip at the café, then read the day-end ledger before you sleep.

10 / 15

THE LINE WE DON'T CROSS

There is no real money in this game.

Every pack, every pull and every wallet in Minthaven runs on money the player earned inside the game. No microtransactions. No ads. No premium currency. No card a credit card can reach.

That isn't a missing feature — it's the argument the whole game is making, and it's the reason a parent can hand this to a ten-year-old who wants to open packs. Premium, one purchase, done.

STATUS

This isn't a concept. The game is playable end-to-end; what remains is production, polish and launch preparation.

Every system in the design document is implemented and playable end to end today — the pack ritual, the market, trading, grading, the duel engine, the tournament ladder, the story lines, the town. What's left is production and polish, not design risk.

BUILT AND PLAYABLE

  • Full game, start to finish, in one file
  • 75 cards in one set, five elements, six rarities
  • Nine locations, twenty-five townsfolk on routines
  • Six story lines, twenty-two achievements
  • Guided tutorial and first-day onboarding
  • Keyboard, mouse and gamepad
  • Ten languages at 100% coverage, guarded by tests
  • Electron desktop shell and the Steam demo gate already in code

WHAT'S LEFT

  • Card illustration: three of seventy-five still to draw
  • Audio: the score is procedural today and wants recording
  • Store assets: capsules, screenshots and the trailer
  • Steamworks wiring: achievements, Cloud, Deck verification
  • Console porting, if we go there

The whole remaining list is production work with known shapes — which is exactly the part a publisher makes faster.

Verified by 65 automated test suites — 5,700+ assertions covering pack odds, pity, the market, trades, tournaments, saves, the duel rules and the map layout. One command runs all of them.
